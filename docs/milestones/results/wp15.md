# WP15: the fill fence after a daemon restart (M2 step 10)

Branch `claude/m2-fence-after-restart`.

## What was wrong

M2 dropped from 111 to 107 checks (found by WP3). Step 10 kills the daemon
three times while two SMB readers wait on a 32 MiB fill.

1. The restarted daemon takes over the held reads at once and fences the
   fill. Ganesha's FSAL has not reconnected yet (on a restart the socket
   server is not even bound yet). With zero connections the fence fails
   (`InvalidateOutcome::all_acked` is false for 0 connections, review B2), the
   edge-local fetch fails, and both readers get EIO. That is fail-closed, but
   the readers should wait and then read the right bytes.
2. The step's "smb-guard: read of non-local bytes" line was missing. This is a
   separate cause: with `[cache]` on (the default), an ONLINE read goes
   through `guard_remote_read`, which never logged the line.
3. The catalog not saying resident follows from 1.

## What changed

- `omnifile-edge/src/core.rs`: a fill's fence (`begin_fill`) now waits up to
  `fsal.reconnect_wait_ms` (new, default 30 000) for the FSALs the edge
  expects: every process whose `/proc/<pid>/exe` is `fsal.required_exe` must
  have a ready connection, and none may be half-way through its handshake.
  - No Ganesha process at all and no connection: the fill is fenced with zero
    connections. A Ganesha starting later changes the epoch, which stops the
    fill, and its FSAL refuses truncates until its resync ends (contract
    §6.1, cases 2 and 3).
  - Which Ganesha runs can't be told (no `required_exe`, `/proc` unreadable,
    a binary that can't be read): zero connections is never accepted.
  - A connected FSAL that does not acknowledge fails the fence at once. The
    bound expiring fails it. A stopped fill or a shutdown ends the wait.
  - The bound is the fill's: `run_fill` retries a broken fence 5 times, and
    a retry within 5 s of the last attempt continues the same wait (review
    on #49). Readers are held at most the bound plus the retries' 3 s of
    backoff.
  - The `/proc` scan runs off the async workers (`spawn_blocking`), reused
    for 500 ms, and not at all while the FSALs connected cover the Ganesha
    set last seen (every M5 chunk fetch fences).
  - Punches and content-version replacements keep the old single-attempt
    fence (B2 unchanged).
- `omnifile-fsal-proto/src/server.rs`: `ready_pids()` and
  `handshaking_connections()`.
- `omnifile-edge/src/guard.rs`: the remote-read path logs "smb-guard: read of
  non-local bytes" when it starts a fetch.
- `docs/contracts/fsal-socket.md` §6.1: the reconnect wait.

NFS during the reconnect window: a disconnected FSAL answers its policy
default (EIO or DELAY) and refuses truncates of `PARTIAL` files; once it is
connected, its `READ_INTENT` is answered from the recall entry (DELAY or
PARK). The marker is the only proof of local bytes, so no read sees zeros.
No FSAL change was needed.

## The soak's 31 EIO reads with no fault active

This fix does not explain them. The soak's daemon log (seed 3125631393) has
no "not every FSAL confirmed the fill fence" line at all.

- All 31 NFS EIOs, and the 4 SMB `NT_STATUS_IO_DEVICE_ERROR`s that
  `events.jsonl` records with no fault active (the summary counts 6), are
  reads of 7 archived files. Each file's errors start during a `fill_disk` fault and go
  on after it ends, for 4 to 46 minutes.
- Trace of one file (538, `wait/soak/d3/f00419.bin`, recall 234):
  1. Its fill fails with ENOSPC while the disk is full ("fill ended without
     the data", 10:22:12 UTC). The service is not told, so the catalog recall
     stays `downloading`.
  2. Every later read asks the service to recall. The service answers with
     recall 234, state `downloading`. `core.rs` (the `RecallResponse::Recall`
     arm, about line 861) sets the entry to FETCHING, but no fill is started.
     Fills start only from the feed's recall rows, and that row doesn't
     change.
  3. The orphan sweep ("FETCHING with no fill running; ending it") ends the
     entry with FAILED, and the readers get EIO. This happened 28 times in
     the soak.
- It is probably the same cause as the 4 recalls the soak found stuck in
  `downloading` (234, 235, 273 and 274 all started during `fill_disk`).
